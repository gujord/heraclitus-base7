    The player

    The figures and the voice. 79 scenes. The voice is generated. Every figure at the end of a shot is a checked still. The picture at any moment depends only on the time in the scene, so pausing, seeking and stepping show the same figure.

    Between scenes. When a scene ends, its last figure stays for two seconds and a card names the next scene. Cancel, space or a click on the figure stops there; the switch in the bar turns autoplay off.

    The description. Under each scene: the passages of the paper it rests on, with what each passage supports, the words the scene defines, and the words it uses that were defined earlier. Where the script states them, also what the viewer can do after the scene and the earlier scenes it rests on.

    The manuscript. View in manuscript opens the paper itself in a window over the player, at the passage a button names, with that passage marked. Playback pauses. The X at the right, or Esc, returns to the same scene and time, still paused. The marked places belong to one PDF, known by its SHA-256 fingerprint; if the PDF in the folder is another one, nothing is marked.

    Check it yourself. On a scene about the checker, beside Share, and on the start page and in the menu. It opens Virtual Python Studio, a window in which your browser runs the checker of the public release, on your machine. It is laid out like a code editor: the files at the left, the open files in the middle, and under them a read-only terminal with the commands and what the programs wrote. The files there are a working copy, in that tab only. A file can be changed, deleted, renamed or made new, to see the checker refuse a changed certificate or to run a program of your own; a run of a changed copy is told as that, and Reset to the release puts every file back. A run there is not the package’s own command, python3 scripts/verify.py: that command starts every step as a process of its own, and a browser has no processes. Here the steps share one Python, and the tests that start a second Python get a second Python interpreter in the page; the terminal lists each such start. Making the certificates again and building the Lean proof are done with the package. Playback pauses; the X at the right, or Esc, returns.

    Looking up a word. A click on an underlined word pauses and shows its definition. From there the scene that defines it can be opened; Back to explanation then returns to the scene and time that were left, also after several lookups in a row.

    The argument here. Nine scenes have a short text under the title that can be read without the voice: what is claimed, what is assumed, why it follows and what it is used for. Under each paragraph are the passages of the paper it rests on; they open the manuscript there. The scenes are marked in the list of chapters.

    The start page. The logo opens it. Its top is the chain of five links that scene 1.6 draws; a link shows the scenes that explain it. Home has a row for each part, Scenes has every scene in the order of the proof, Playlists has the links and the parts as lists, and the magnifying glass searches the words of all scenes. The line under a scene's title names the link the scene belongs to. This shows where a scene belongs, not every dependence between results.

    The menu. The three lines at the top left open it: the tabs of the start page, where you left off, each link of the chain, each part, the manuscript, Help and About. On a wide start page it stands beside the page; elsewhere it comes over the page. Appearance, in the menu, sets the page light or dark, or lets it follow your device; the figures and the manuscript look the same in both.

    Share. Shows the address of the scene, from its beginning or from the time shown, to copy. The address names the scene and a shot, for example #7.2/5, or a time, #7.2@15.4.
